The Story

Better Dayz was Tupac Shakur’s posthumous 27-track double album, released on Amaru Records, Tha Row Records and Interscope on 26 November 2002 — six years after his September 1996 death — reaching No. 5 on the Billboard 200 and later certified 3x Platinum.

Patchwerk logged four sessions connected to it, spanning three days in September 2002, two months before release. Leslie Brathwaite mixed in Studio 9000 on 23 and 24 September for Amaru Records, Jazze Pha producing; engineer Claudio tracked new Outlawz vocals in Studio 995 on 24 and 25 September — “Anyway” and “Changed Man,” the group back in the booth to build around Tupac’s vocals six years after he was gone. The 24th is the one date the two rooms share.

One clean caveat belongs here: no song titles were entered in the main field on either mix sheet in Studio 9000, so the Better Dayz attribution rests on the label, the date and the personnel on the sheets, not a direct title match — call it a Medium-confidence read. The strongest link comes from a different sheet: a handwritten footnote on the Studio 995 “Changed Man” report reads “2Pac → ‘U Can Call Me’ → ‘Fair Exchange,’” naming both songs together, and both correspond to tracks on the finished album — though the reading itself is flagged uncertain on the sheet. Each Studio 9000 mix sheet separately guesses at a single title of its own, also marked uncertain.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was 2Pac’s Better Dayz mixed?

Session reports place work connected to 2Pac’s 2002 album Better Dayz at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta, billed to his mother’s label, Amaru Records. Four sessions, all in a single week of September 2002 — two months before the album’s 26 November release — split between Studio 9000, where Leslie Brathwaite mixed, and Studio 995, where the Outlawz tracked new vocals.

How Sure Are We

Is it confirmed that these Patchwerk sessions are for Better Dayz?

Medium confidence. No song titles were entered in the main field on Patchwerk’s two Studio 9000 mix sheets from this week, so the match to Better Dayz rests on the label (Amaru Records), the date (two months pre-release) and the personnel (Leslie Brathwaite, Jazze Pha) rather than a direct title match. The clearest link is a handwritten footnote on the Studio 995 “Changed Man” sheet, naming both “U Can Call Me” and “Fair Exchange” — songs that made the finished record — though the reading itself is flagged uncertain on the sheet.

The Outlawz

Were the Outlawz recording at Patchwerk during the Better Dayz sessions?

Yes. Engineer Claudio tracked new Outlawz vocals in Patchwerk’s Studio 995 for two songs logged as “Anyway” on 24 September 2002 and “Changed Man” on 25 September — the 24th also the date Leslie Brathwaite was mixing in Studio 9000 down the hall. “Changed Man,” produced by Jazze Pha, is on the released album.

2PAC feat. Jagged Edge

Does a Patchwerk sheet credit “2PAC feat. Jagged Edge”?

Yes — the 24 September 2002 mix sheet in Studio 9000 lists the artist as “2PAC feat. Jagged Edge,” Leslie Brathwaite engineering with Cory Williams assisting, a nine-hour session billed to Amaru Records. No Jagged Edge feature appears on the released Better Dayz tracklist, so the pairing may have been mixed and shelved, or belongs to another Amaru-era session; the sheet is included here for the record.

Jazze Pha

Did Jazze Pha work on Better Dayz at Patchwerk?

Patchwerk’s September 2002 sheets list Jazze Pha as producer on three of the four sessions, and the released album credits him on several remixes, including “Changed Man (Jazze Pha Remix)” and “U Can Call (Jazze Pha Remix).” Both title matches support the Medium-confidence read above, even though neither was entered cleanly in a sheet’s main Song Title field.

Why it matters

Six years after Tupac Shakur’s death, Amaru Records was still building albums from his vault — and still booking rooms at Patchwerk to do it. The paperwork doesn’t carry a title for what was mixed those two days in September 2002, only a label, a date and a crew that match the record it became.

Engineering credits shown are drawn directly from Patchwerk’s own session logs for this record. Album attribution here is Medium confidence: it rests on label, date and personnel rather than song titles logged on the sheets themselves. A separate October 2003 Outlawz session (“One Day @ A Time”) belongs to Loyal to the Game and is not included here.

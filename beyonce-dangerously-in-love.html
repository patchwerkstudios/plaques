The Story

Beyoncé’s solo debut album, Dangerously in Love, arrived on Columbia Records on 24 June 2003, debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, and has been certified 7× Platinum by the RIAA.

Patchwerk holds only two session reports naming Beyoncé, and they were both filed on the same night: 11 September 2002, in Studio 995, with engineer Mike Wilson working at a rate of forty dollars an hour and Justin Phillips assisting. What makes the pair worth keeping isn’t the overlap — it’s that Patchwerk billed them to two different labels.

The first ran 5pm to 9pm, billed to Elektra Records, producer credited only as “Missy,” the project description line just reading “Pro Tools” — but the sheet’s notes name the song plainly as “Ain’t Nothing Out There”, the same song Missy Elliott had tracked her own vocal on three days earlier for Under Construction, released under the title “Nothing Out There for Me.” The studio rented in an M149 tube microphone for the date, $125 from Patchwerk itself.

The second ran 9pm to midnight, billed to Columbia Records, producer credited to Beyoncé herself, three vocal hours logged for a song title written plainly on the sheet: “Gift From Virgo.” It survived onto the finished album, track fourteen on Dangerously in Love.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was Beyoncé’s Dangerously in Love recorded?

Beyoncé worked on Dangerously in Love at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta, Georgia. Patchwerk’s own paperwork documents two vocal sessions in Studio 995 on the night of 11 September 2002, both engineered by Mike Wilson at forty dollars an hour with Justin Phillips assisting.

Two Labels, One Night

Why are there two different Patchwerk session reports for Beyoncé from the same day?

Patchwerk filed two separate session reports for Beyoncé on 11 September 2002 because the two sessions were billed to two different record labels. The 5–9pm session was billed to Elektra Records, and the 9pm–midnight session that followed it, in the same room with the same engineer, was billed to Columbia Records — Beyoncé’s own label for Dangerously in Love.

The Song

What song did Beyoncé record at Patchwerk that made Dangerously in Love?

“Gift from Virgo” is the song named on Beyoncé’s Patchwerk session report from 11 September 2002, and it made the finished album as track fourteen on Dangerously in Love. The Columbia-billed session ran three hours, 9pm to midnight, with the song title written plainly rather than left off the sheet.

The Missy Elliott Connection

Is Beyoncé’s Patchwerk session connected to Missy Elliott’s Under Construction?

Yes — Beyoncé’s Elektra-billed Patchwerk session on 11 September 2002 logs the song title “Ain’t Nothing Out There,” the same song Missy Elliott had tracked at Patchwerk three days earlier, on 8 September, under the title “Nothing Out There for Me.” Missy’s own session report that day releases audio copies to both “Missy + Beyonce” by name, placing Beyoncé in the building during that session too.

The Engineer

Who engineered Beyoncé’s sessions at Patchwerk?

Mike Wilson engineered both of Beyoncé’s logged Patchwerk sessions, at a billed rate of $40 an hour, with Justin Phillips assisting. Both sessions ran back-to-back in Studio 995 on 11 September 2002.

Why it matters

One night, one room, one engineer at forty dollars an hour — and two different major labels each holding an invoice for the same artist. The Elektra sheet, filed the same night as the Columbia session that produced Gift from Virgo, connects straight to Missy Elliott’s own Patchwerk sessions for Under Construction that same week.

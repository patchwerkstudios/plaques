The Story

D4L came out of Bankhead — Fabo, Shawty Lo, Stuntman, Mook B and DeMond “Ready” Days — making party rap built on 808s and call-and-response, signed to Shawty Lo’s own D4L Records through Dee Money Entertainment and Asylum Records. Down for Life arrived 8 November 2005, debuted at No. 22 on the Billboard 200, and was certified Gold; its lead single “Laffy Taffy” did something almost no one predicted, going to No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 on 14 January 2006 — one of the first singles to top the chart on the strength of ringtone sales — and was certified three-times Platinum.

Patchwerk’s own daily session reports put the album’s finishing work almost evenly between two rooms: Studio 995, where engineer Mike Wilson mixed track after track for D-Money Records from May 2005 into March 2006 — his last logged session for the album billed to Atlantic Records, not D-Money — and the dub room, which stayed just as busy burning promo CDs, fixing corrupted remix files at 4am, and, seven months after release, bringing in Rick Ross to add a verse.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was D4L’s Down for Life recorded?

D4L recorded Down for Life at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta, Georgia. Patchwerk’s own daily session reports document 36 sessions for the album between May 2005 and July 2006, split almost evenly between Studio 995, where Mike Wilson mixed for D-Money Records, and the dub room, which handled copy, backup and remix-fix work — plus two tracking sessions in Studio 9000.

Laffy Taffy

How early was “Laffy Taffy” recorded before it became a hit?

D4L’s “Laffy Taffy” was first mixed at Patchwerk Recording Studios on 2 May 2005, more than six months before Down for Life released on 8 November 2005 and eight months before the song hit No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 on 14 January 2006. The 2 May session report lists it simply as a mix fee alongside another track, “Flexin,” with no hint that it was about to become one of the first ringtone-era chart-toppers.

Rick Ross

Did Rick Ross record a verse for D4L at Patchwerk?

Yes — Rick Ross tracked his verse for D4L’s “I’m Da Man” at Patchwerk Recording Studios on 31 May 2006, seven months after Down for Life was released. The session report lists the artist as “D4L / Rick Ross,” the project as “Tracking Rick Ross verse,” engineer Kori Anders and assistant Denny Ogle, in Studio 9000 from 1:45 to 3:30pm.

The Engineer

Who mixed D4L’s Down for Life?

Mike Wilson mixed Down for Life at Patchwerk Recording Studios, engineering 16 of the album’s 36 logged sessions — more than any other engineer on the record — almost all of it in Studio 995 for D-Money Records, running from 2 May 2005 to 16 March 2006, when his final logged session was billed to Atlantic Records instead. Dub room engineers J. Stevenson, Denny Ogle and Tony Rey handled the copy, backup and remix-editing work that kept the singles moving after release.

The Setback

Did anything go wrong recording D4L’s Down for Life?

Yes — a Patchwerk session report from 13 October 2005 describes a scramble to finish the “Laffy Taffy” remix: dub room engineer Denny Ogle received files that “didn’t work,” had to drive to D4L’s own studio to get the right ones, then lined everything up, bounced a clean edit and made backups — a four-and-a-half-hour overnight session, midnight to 4:30am.

Why it matters

Down for Life is proof a No. 1 record can start as an ordinary Monday mix session. “Laffy Taffy” was tracked at Patchwerk six months before it became one of the first ringtone-era chart-toppers — and when Rick Ross needed a verse the following spring, Patchwerk is where he cut it.

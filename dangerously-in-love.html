The Story

Dangerously in Love was Beyoncé’s debut solo album, made during Destiny’s Child’s hiatus. She took a far wider creative role than before — co-writing most of the songs, interviewing producers in person, and weighing in on every mix. Columbia executives reportedly told her the album had no hits. It produced five charting singles.

“Crazy in Love,” built on Rich Harrison’s Chi-Lites horn sample, became the song of summer 2003; “Baby Boy” with Sean Paul spent nine weeks at No. 1. The album debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 and has since been certified 7× Platinum, selling over 11 million copies worldwide.

At the 2004 Grammys it won five awards, including Best Contemporary R&B Album — announcing the arrival of a solo superstar and cementing the Beyoncé–Jay-Z partnership in the public eye.

The Patchwerk Connection

Recorded across studios in New York and Atlanta, Dangerously in Love lists Patchwerk among its Atlanta recording homes. The album’s strongest Atlanta ties run through “Hip Hop Star,” featuring OutKast’s Big Boi and Sleepy Brown and engineered by Young Guru.

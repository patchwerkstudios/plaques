The Story

“Elevators (Me & You)” was the lead single from OutKast’s 1996 sophomore album ATLiens, a departure from the group’s debut Southernplayalisticadillacmuzik built on reverb-heavy percussion, spacey electric guitar and a chorus voiced by Big Boi, Sleepy Brown, Debra Killings & the Jazzyfatnastees. André and Big Boi trade verses about the climb from writing and performing around Atlanta to the world stage — “…coming up, slammin’ Cadillac doors” — while the hook admits that success hasn’t erased the bill-to-bill grind underneath it. It became a defining record for Southern hip-hop, the sound that made good on André’s later claim that “the South got something to say.”

Patchwerk holds two of its own session reports naming the song, both quoted in full below. The first, dated 25 November 1995, is the oldest dated session sheet in the studio’s whole archive — the record behind Patchwerk’s very first plaque.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was OutKast’s “Elevators (Me & You)” recorded?

OutKast recorded “Elevators (Me & You)” at Patchwerk Recording Studios’ original home at 995 McMillan Street in Atlanta. Patchwerk’s own daily session report places the song being written and tracked there on 25 November 1995 — the oldest dated session in the studio’s whole archive, and the record behind Patchwerk’s very first plaque.

The Session

What does Patchwerk’s 1995 session report for “Elevators” actually show?

The sheet for 25 November 1995 is extraordinary: it records “Elevators” being written in the room, not just tracked. Engineer Khalifani logged a ten-hour date for client “Outkast/ONP,” 4:00pm to 2:20am, in three blocks — “Writing” from 5 to 10pm (five hours), “Tracked Elevator” from 10pm to 1:30am (three and a half hours), then “Mixed (Rough) Son[g]” from 1:30 to 2:00am (half an hour). The project description, in the studio’s own words: “Wrote music for ‘Elevators’, tracked music for elevators w/ live bass (Rustin). Made a rough mix and recorded it to cassette.” The Studer’s tape-head hour counter reads 566.1 at the top of the sheet and 568.7 at the bottom. Both engineer and client signed the same night.

The Second Sheet

Is there a second Patchwerk session report for “Elevators”?

Yes — a second sheet from 2 February 1996, engineer Derrick Williams, logs a title written plainly as “Elevators” from 2:00 to 3:30pm inside a five-hour date for client “ONP” that ran noon to 5pm and closed out with “Made Show Tapes” to one 60-minute DAT. The other three titles on that sheet are only partly legible and the artist isn’t written down anywhere on it, so honestly: this is very likely the same OutKast song, but the sheet itself doesn’t say so. No signatures were collected that day.

The Engineers

Who engineered OutKast’s “Elevators (Me & You)” at Patchwerk?

Khalifani engineered the founding session on 25 November 1995, writing and tracking the music with live bass from Rustin and cutting the first rough mix to cassette. Derrick Williams, with a second engineer whose name is only partly legible on the sheet, logged the follow-up date on 2 February 1996.

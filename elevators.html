The Story

“Elevators (Me & You)” was the lead single from ATLiens, the 1996 album where André 3000 and Big Boi stepped out from under Organized Noize to produce for themselves — and this hypnotic, bass-heavy track was one of the first they built on their own. Its slowed, spacey minimalism was a deliberate break from the live-funk of their debut and helped define the cosmic “ATLien” sound.

The lyrics turned inward, reflecting on OutKast’s rise from local Atlanta hopefuls to national artists and the strange gravity of new fame and money. It became their biggest hit to that point: No. 12 on the Billboard Hot 100, No. 1 on Hot Rap Tracks, and a Platinum certification from the RIAA.

Patchwerk engineer Jarvis Blackshear recalls André cutting his vocals for “Elevators” — along with several other ATLiens tracks — in a single burst of about four hours, knocked out on a $500 bet to be finished by eight o’clock.

“We did the vocals to all those songs in about four hours.”

Today it stands as a landmark of Southern hip-hop’s mid-90s artistic leap, still studied by engineers for its warm, murky low end — the sound of Atlanta finding its own voice.

From Patchwerk’s Archive

The studio’s own session logs capture “Elevators” being built in-house. On November 25, 1995, engineer Khalifani recorded the track — tracking the music with live bass and cutting the first rough mix to cassette — in an overnight session that ran from 4 p.m. to 2:20 a.m. to the Studer 2-inch tape, signed off in the studio’s own hand on this daily session report.

The Patchwerk Connection

Patchwerk’s session logs capture “Elevators” being built in-house. On Nov 25, 1995, engineer Khalifani recorded the music — tracking live bass (Rustin) and cutting the first rough mix to cassette; engineer Jarvis Blackshear later recorded André’s vocals, and the record appears again in the logs on Feb 2, 1996 with engineer Derrick Williams. A genuine piece of the studio’s earliest history.

The Story

Def Jam signed Young Jeezy off the strength of his self-released mixtapes and manager Kevin “Coach K” Lee, who believed the street rapper could cross over; Def Jam executive Shakir Stewart brought the deal in, and Jeezy brought his own label, Corporate Thugz Entertainment, and producer Shawty Redd, whose stripped-down, minor-key beats defined the record. Let’s Get It: Thug Motivation 101 arrived 26 July 2005 on Def Jam and CTE, debuted at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 on 172,000 first-week copies, was certified 2× Platinum, and has since sold more than two million copies — a blueprint for the Atlanta trap that followed.

Let’s Get It: Thug Motivation 101 is the most heavily documented album in Patchwerk’s archive — 130 session reports running from a two-hour vocal date in December 2003 to 12 June 2005, when Def Jam’s Shakir Stewart signed for every last piece of material and took it with him, six weeks before the 26 July release. No other record in the building has this much paper behind it.

The paper trail shows a genuine two-engineer, two-room record. Nico Solis started the sessions in January 2004 as Leslie Brathwaite’s assistant on the mix side, then by April had become Jeezy’s own tracking engineer, running almost every vocal date in Studio 995 through 2004 and 2005. Leslie Brathwaite stayed the record’s dedicated mixer, working almost exclusively out of Studio 9000 — nineteen of his twenty logged sessions ran there. On at least one night, 13 October 2004, the two of them passed “Air Force Ones” back and forth inside a single day: written from 2pm, tracked by Nico from 10pm, handed to Leslie to mix at midnight — whose own sheet admits, “Leslie did not finish the mix.”

Guests came through too — Bun B and Stat Quo tracking “Trap or Die” verses in November 2004, Fabolous cutting a freestyle that never made the album, Mannie Fresh in the building through the spring of 2005 for “And Then What.” Coach K and Shakir Stewart are named again and again as the ones signing for CDs and DATs at the front desk; Patchwerk’s sheets name all nineteen of the album’s songs.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was Young Jeezy’s Let’s Get It: Thug Motivation 101 recorded?

Young Jeezy recorded Let’s Get It: Thug Motivation 101 at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta, Georgia. Patchwerk’s own daily session reports document 129 sessions for the album between December 2003 and June 2005 — more than for any other record in the studio’s archive — spread across Studio 995, Studio 9000 and the dub room at 1094 Hemphill Avenue. The album also used two other Atlanta rooms, Dirty South Studios and Stankonia, but Patchwerk carries the largest paper trail of the three.

The Engineers

Who engineered Let’s Get It: Thug Motivation 101?

Nico Solis and Leslie Brathwaite engineered most of Let’s Get It: Thug Motivation 101 at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta — Solis tracking Young Jeezy’s vocals in Studio 995, Brathwaite mixing in Studio 9000, where nineteen of his twenty logged sessions ran. Nico started the record in January 2004 as Leslie’s assistant before becoming Jeezy’s own tracking engineer that April. Steve Fisher engineered a dozen more sessions, including the album’s longest, and Kori Anders logged 27 sessions in total — mostly as assistant to Solis and Brathwaite, but stepping up as lead engineer on at least four, including the tab’s final session on 12 June 2005.

Two Rooms

Did Young Jeezy’s album really use two different rooms at Patchwerk?

Yes — Patchwerk’s sheets show a genuine two-room production, tracking in 995 and mixing in 9000, that occasionally closed the loop in a single day. On 13 October 2004, “Air Force Ones” was written in Studio 9000 from 2pm, tracked there by Nico Solis from 10pm, and handed to Leslie Brathwaite to mix from midnight — whose sheet notes plainly, “Leslie did not finish the mix.” Twenty-five of the album’s 100 studio dates carry more than one session report, evidence of a record built in relay rather than a straight line.

The Guests

Did Bun B record his verse for “Trap or Die” at Patchwerk?

Yes. Bun B and Stat Quo tracked verses for “Trap or Die” at Patchwerk Recording Studios on 16 November 2004, alongside Shawty Redd and Jeezy himself, in a ten-and-a-half-hour session engineered by Steve Fisher. Four audio CDs went out that night, one each to Jeezy, Stat Quo, Bun B and Shawty Redd — though the version that reached the album kept only Bun B’s verse. Fabolous also tracked two guest vocals at Patchwerk on 26 August 2004, for a freestyle built on “Problems / Everybody Move” that never made the final tracklist.

Mixtape Culture

Was Young Jeezy’s Gangsta Grillz mixtape also made at Patchwerk?

Yes — on 7 July 2004, Patchwerk’s dub room hosted a session for DJ Drama’s Gangsta Grillz tape “The Streetz Iz Watchin,” mixed, mastered and dubbed to DAT and CD by Steve Fisher in the same rooms, and often the same weeks, as the retail album. The mixtape circuit and the major-label record were built side by side.

Why it matters

Let’s Get It: Thug Motivation 101 carries more Patchwerk paperwork than any other record in the archive — 130 session reports across nineteen months, two engineers running two rooms in relay, and executives from Coach K to Shakir Stewart signing for masters in person. It is the sound of a label being built one session report at a time.

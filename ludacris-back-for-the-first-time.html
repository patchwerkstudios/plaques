The Story

Before he was Ludacris, Chris Bridges was “Chris Lova Lova,” a mix-show DJ on Atlanta’s Hot 107.9 who self-released his debut, Incognegro, in 1999 through his own label, Disturbing tha Peace. His follow-up, Back for the First Time, released 17 October 2000 on Def Jam South / Disturbing tha Peace, debuted at No. 4 on the Billboard 200 and was certified Triple Platinum.

The earliest sheets in this run — five dates in January 2000 — are logged in Room B wherever the room is filled in at all, cycling through three different engineers (J. Butler, Dale “Rambo” Ramsey and J. Blackshear) as Ludacris tracked songs one at a time for cash, including What’s Your Fantasy’s hooks and beat on 10 January. The sessions don’t settle into Studio 995 with engineer Mike Wilson until 3 February; by Mines Like That on 16 February — the first sheet where both the Producer and Client fields simply read “Chris Luva” — that pairing is locked in, and it holds through a run of $200–$800 mix fees into March as the DTP catalogue got sequenced into shape.

That changes on 19 August 2000. The client field on a “Stick ’Em Up” rough-mix sheet flips for the first time to “Def Jam South” — the major distributing Disturbing tha Peace had by then signed. It didn’t stick immediately: the billing bounces between “Chris Lova” and “Def Jam South” through September, on songs like the Neptunes-produced Southern Hospitality, before settling on the label for good by the November show-tape run — a month after the album shipped.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was Ludacris’s Back for the First Time recorded?

Ludacris recorded Back for the First Time at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta, Georgia. Patchwerk’s own daily session reports document 30 sessions tied to the album between January and November 2000 — the earliest cash-paid dates in Room B, then the bulk of the record from February on in Studio 995 with engineer Mike Wilson, running through the month after the record’s 17 October release.

The Billing

Why do Patchwerk’s session sheets for Ludacris say “Chris Luva” instead of his name?

“Chris Luva” — also written “Chris Lova” — is Ludacris’s own on-air radio handle, Chris Lova Lova, from his years as a mix-show DJ on Atlanta’s Hot 107.9. Ten of the thirty Patchwerk sheets for this album list “Chris Luva,” “Chris Lova” or “Chris Bridges” as both producer and client, and the client field alone carries one of those names on eighteen sheets total — meaning Ludacris, still running his independent label Disturbing tha Peace, was booking and paying for his own studio time before Def Jam South’s money arrived.

The Handoff

When did Def Jam South take over paying for Ludacris’s studio time at Patchwerk?

Def Jam South first appears as the client on a Patchwerk session report dated 19 August 2000, a rough mix of “Stick ’Em Up” engineered by J. Blackshear, produced by Shondrae. It wasn’t an immediate, permanent switch — the billing keeps alternating between “Chris Lova” and “Def Jam South” through September, on sessions for “Ho” and “Southern Hospitality” among others — before settling on the label by the last sheet in the window, a show-tape session on 16 November, a month after the album’s release.

The Producers

Who produced Back for the First Time at Patchwerk?

Patchwerk’s session reports name Shondrae (credited on the album as Bangladesh) as producer of the “Stick ’Em Up” and “Ho” sessions, and the Neptunes as producer of the 19 September 2000 “Southern Hospitality” mix — both credited on the released album. Mike Wilson engineered the great majority of the sessions, with J. Butler, J. Blackshear, Ismel Ramos, Khalifani and Dale “Rambo” Ramsey also logging dates.

The Singles

Was “What’s Your Fantasy” recorded at Patchwerk?

Yes — Back for the First Time’s lead single, “What’s Your Fantasy,” was worked on at Patchwerk Recording Studios across five separate sessions in 2000. The earliest, on 10 January, tracked the hooks and beat under the client name “Criss / DTP” — Ludacris’s own Disturbing tha Peace. Later sessions in March, August and September mixed the song and its remix, which added Trina, Shawnna and Foxy Brown.

Cut But Not Kept

Did Ludacris record songs at Patchwerk that didn’t make Back for the First Time?

Yes. Early sessions at Patchwerk tracked three songs that never appeared on the finished sixteen-track album: Midnite Train (3 January, Room B), Come See Me (4 January, Room B) and Mines Like That (16 February, Studio 995). All three came back for mixing in March 2000 — Come See Me on 6 March, then Midnite Train and Mines Like That together on 10 March in Studio 995 — all engineered by Mike Wilson, before the record’s tracklist locked in.

Why it matters

The paperwork for Back for the First Time is a receipt for an artist’s ascension: for seven months the client line reads “Chris Luva,” a radio DJ paying cash for his own record. Then, on one August afternoon, it reads “Def Jam South” for the first time — and hip hop had a new major-label star before the album had even shipped.

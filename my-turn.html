The Story

Lil Baby’s second studio album My Turn arrived 28 February 2020 on Quality Control and Motown, debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, returned to the top repeatedly through the year, and finished as the most-consumed album in America in 2020.

Patchwerk’s own session reports document 31 sessions billed to Motown Records with Lil Baby as the artist, running from 16 July to 15 December 2019 — the months the record was being made. Thirty of the thirty-one are in Studio 9000. It reads less like a series of bookings than a room held open: night after night, the same studio, start times of 9pm, 10pm, midnight, 1am, 2am.

Latrez McClam engineered thirteen of them, more than anyone; Leonard Nalls six, Alverne Emmanuel, Evan Melville and Daniel “Tugg” Tuggerson three each. The drive paths written on the sheets read like a filing system built around one client — “9000 Work Drive → Verne → Lil Baby,” “9000 – Leonard – session – Baby.”

Most sheets log the work generically — “Production,” “Beats,” “S1 / S2 / S3” — which is how a room being used as a workshop gets written down. But one names an album track outright, and one records the album being played back in the building eleven weeks before it shipped.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was Lil Baby’s My Turn recorded?

Lil Baby worked at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta throughout the making of My Turn. Patchwerk’s own daily session reports document 31 sessions billed to Motown Records between 16 July and 15 December 2019 — thirty of them in Studio 9000 — in the five months before the album’s 28 February 2020 release.

The Album Track

Was “Consistent” from My Turn recorded at Patchwerk?

Patchwerk’s session report for 29 July 2019 logs “Tracking: Consistent” in Studio 9000 — Consistent is track twelve on My Turn, cut seven months before the album’s release. Engineer Alverne Emmanuel ran the session from 9pm to 10am, thirteen hours, logging two more items alongside it: “Two Too Many Ft Lil Baby” and “Idea (Frank Lucas).” The sheet records where the work went afterwards, in a line that dates the session precisely: “Backup sent to: baby4pf@icloud.com.”

The Playback

Did Lil Baby play My Turn back at Patchwerk before it came out?

Yes. A Patchwerk session report dated 6 December 2019 logs ten hours in Studio 9000, 10pm to 8am, under the heading “Listen to songs from Album” — with a second line reading “Tracked By Matt — Features.” Engineer Daniel “Tugg” Tuggerson ran it. That is eleven weeks before My Turn was released: the record being heard end to end, and features still being added, in the room where much of it was made.

The Engineers

Who engineered Lil Baby’s Patchwerk sessions?

Latrez McClam engineered more of Lil Baby’s Patchwerk sessions than anyone — thirteen of the thirty-one logged — at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta. Leonard Nalls ran six, and Alverne Emmanuel, Evan Melville and Daniel “Tugg” Tuggerson three apiece, with Matt and Anthony “DJ Doo” Jones also logging dates. One July sheet notes the client arriving at 12:30 with his own engineer — the house staff running the room around him.

The Longest Night

What was the longest Lil Baby session at Patchwerk?

The longest single Lil Baby session in Patchwerk’s records ran fourteen hours — 8pm on 22 October 2019 to 10am the next morning, in Studio 9000. The artist line reads “Lil Baby – Money Bagg Yo,” the song logged is “Uh Oh,” and an M-Audio Key Rig 49 was rented in for the date. The drive path written at the bottom names all three: “9000 – Leonard – session – bagg x lil baby x uh oh.” Moneybagg Yo also appears on a 3 September sheet alongside Lil Keed.

The Producers

Which producers worked with Lil Baby at Patchwerk?

Patchwerk’s sheets name several producers across Lil Baby’s 2019 sessions in Studio 9000 — most notably ATL Jacob, logged on 20 August 2019 under a project line that simply reads “Baby x Jacob.” Also named: Geno and MAT across two consecutive September nights, Quay on 10 September, and Twlysted Genius on 7 December.

Why it matters

Thirty-one sheets, one room, five months. The album that would be the most-listened-to record in America in 2020 was being built overnight in Studio 9000, and the paperwork was filed the same as any other night’s work.

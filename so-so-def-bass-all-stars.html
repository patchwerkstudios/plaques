The Story

Before trap had a name, Jermaine Dupri’s So So Def put a different Atlanta sound on wax: bass music, the 808-driven, call-and-response strain that ran Southern radio and skating rinks through the mid-’90s. Between 1996 and 1998 the label released three volumes of So So Def Bass All-Stars, compiling the DJs, producers and one-off vocalists working that lane — Ghost Town DJ’s, Playa Poncho, Trigga Man, Zae’, and a young Jonathan “Lil Jon” Smith arranging, mixing and producing across nearly every track.

Of the 36 session reports that touch this catalogue, one names a volume outright: 13 August 1998, engineer Mike Wilson at the board, logging “Editing / snippets” with a project line reading “All Stars Vol 3.” The rest bill the client simply as So So Def — the label, in its bass era, working out of these rooms week after week.

What the sheets do show is a label building its sound in real time. My Boo, the Ghost Town DJ’s single that became the series’ biggest hit, was mixed and re-tracked at Patchwerk across three sessions in August and September 1996. That December, a run of overnight dates tracked, mixed and remixed Lil Jon’s own early productions — Booty Time, Booty Call, Bad — years before he had a solo career of his own. Freak It got an edit session in March 1997 and a remix mix that September; Drop Them Bo’s on Em, Mind Busters and Trick Bustas filled out the run through 1998 and into 1999.

The Recording

The Studio

Was So So Def Bass All-Stars recorded at Patchwerk Recording Studios?

So So Def’s Bass All-Stars compilation series was assembled in part at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta, whose own daily session reports document 36 sessions billed to the label between August 1996 and May 1999. One of those sheets names a specific volume — 13 August 1998, engineer Mike Wilson, logging work on “All Stars Vol 3” — while the rest are billed to the label across its bass-era catalogue.

My Boo

Where was Ghost Town DJ’s “My Boo” mixed?

Ghost Town DJ’s “My Boo,” the breakout single from So So Def Bass All-Stars Vol. 1, was mixed at Patchwerk Recording Studios on 10 August 1996 by engineer Carlos Glover with Jarvis Blackshear assisting — a 5.75-hour session logged simply as “Mixed ‘My Boo’ Remix” for client So So Def. Additional tracking and a recall-and-remix followed on 1 and 3 September 1996.

Lil Jon’s Early Sessions

Did Lil Jon work at Patchwerk before he was a solo star?

Yes — Patchwerk’s session reports place Jonathan “Lil Jon” Smith’s music in the building as early as December 1996, years before his own albums, tracking and mixing “Booty Time” / “Booty Call” over four sessions that month — two of them billed as “Lil John/So So Def,” the others billed as “So So Def” alone and “So So Def/ZAE.” The March and September 1997 sheets add “Freak It,” credited to Lil Jon & the East Side Boyz, including its edit session and a later remix mix.

The Volume 3 Sheet

Which Patchwerk session names So So Def Bass All-Stars Vol. 3 directly?

A 13 August 1998 session report names “All Stars Vol 3” directly: engineer Mike Wilson logged three hours of “Editing / snippets” under that project line. Every other session in the run, going back to 1996, bills the client simply as “So So Def.”

The Span

How long did Patchwerk work on the So So Def Bass All-Stars series?

Patchwerk’s session reports touching the Bass All-Stars series run nearly three years, from a “My Boo” remix mix on 10 August 1996 to a one-hour “Break You Off” drop-off session on 15 May 1999 — well past Vol. III’s 1998 release, suggesting Patchwerk’s relationship with the label’s bass catalogue outlasted the numbered volumes themselves.

Deeper Catalogue

What other So So Def bass tracks were cut at Patchwerk?

Beyond the marquee singles, Patchwerk’s sheets name a run of deeper-catalogue bass tracks recorded for So So Def: “Mind Busters” (28 August 1997), “Drop Them Bo’s on Em” (10 February 1998), the “When Will I” bass remix (three sessions, September–November 1998) and “Trick Bustas” (27 January 1999) — the B-side and mixtape material that filled out the label’s bass catalogue between the numbered compilations.

Why it matters

Long before trap had a name, this is where Atlanta’s bass sound got made — the same rooms, six years before Trap Muzik. Lil Jon’s name is on a Patchwerk sheet in December 1996, four years before his own solo career took off, tracking bass records for the label that gave him his start.

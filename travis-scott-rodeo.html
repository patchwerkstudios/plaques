The Story

Rodeo, Travis Scott’s debut studio album, shipped 4 September 2015 on Grand Hustle through Epic Records and debuted at No. 3 on the Billboard 200.

Patchwerk’s own session reports place almost the entire album camp in one eight-day stretch, 21–28 February 2015, running three rooms at once. The building split by function, loosely: Studio 1019 ran beat-making and listening sessions, often overnight, off the artist’s own drives; Studio 995 logged mostly listening sessions too, with tracking work on only two of its six dates; Studio 9000 carried the heaviest load, splitting evenly between tracking and mixing, with Alex Tumay engineering nearly every session there and Nick Bassani assisting across the whole camp. A rented Sony C800 microphone shows up on sheet after sheet in 9000, and Studio 995’s sheets separately list a rented Juno 106 and Korg Triton.

The songs were logged under names that wouldn’t survive to the tracklist — Dinosaurs, Be Me, See Me, Company, Tell Em Lies, Mine, Bruh, Diplo, Avatar, ABB, Fiyahyah — alongside the ones that made it, Nightcrawler, Oh My Dis Side and Maria I’m Drunk (logged simply as “Drunk”). Young Thug is in the room for two of the tracking sessions.

The Recording

The Studio

Where was Travis Scott’s Rodeo recorded?

Travis Scott recorded much of his debut album Rodeo at Patchwerk Recording Studios in Atlanta, Georgia. Patchwerk’s own daily session reports document 21 sessions for an Epic Records camp run between 21 and 28 February 2015, working three rooms at once — Studio 1019, Studio 995 and Studio 9000.

The Rooms

How did Patchwerk’s three studios split the work on Rodeo?

Patchwerk ran Rodeo’s February 2015 camp across three rooms, though the split wasn’t as clean as a simple divide. Studio 1019 handled beat-making and listening, frequently through the night; Studio 995 logged mostly listening sessions too, with tracking work on only two of its six dates; and Studio 9000 carried the bulk of the tracking and mixing, splitting evenly between the two, with Alex Tumay engineering nearly every session there and Nick Bassani assisting across the entire camp, in all three rooms.

The Engineers

Who engineered Travis Scott’s Rodeo at Patchwerk?

Alex Tumay engineered nearly every Studio 9000 session for Rodeo at Patchwerk Recording Studios — a mix of tracking and mixing dates, split about evenly — working alongside an engineer logged as “Jordan” (also written Jordan Louis and Jordan Lewis across different sheets), with Nick Bassani assisting on fifteen of the camp’s twenty-two sessions. A rented Sony C800 microphone appears on the 9000 sheets throughout the week, billed at $180 a session.

Young Thug

Is Young Thug on Travis Scott’s Rodeo?

Yes — Young Thug is named directly on two of Patchwerk’s Rodeo session reports, both in Studio 9000 with the artist field reading “Travis Scott/Young Thug.” On 21 February 2015 the pair tracked songs logged as Dinosaurs, Be Me, See Me and I Mean; on 23 February they tracked Drunk — released as “Maria I’m Drunk,” featuring Justin Bieber and Young Thug — alongside two more working titles, Mine and Bruh.

Working Titles

What were the working titles for songs on Rodeo?

Patchwerk’s session reports log at least ten Rodeo songs under working titles the tracks would lose before release: Dinosaurs, Be Me, See Me, Company, Tell Em Lies, Mine, Bruh, Diplo, Avatar, ABB and Fiyahyah. One sheet even notes in the engineer’s own hand that the session titles are “Not real names of songs (Potential Names).” The studio’s own paperwork ties “Fiyahyah” to the released track “Flying High.” None of the other nine working titles is identifiable on the finished sixteen-track album.

Why it matters

Rodeo was built as a camp, not a string of dates — three Patchwerk rooms running at once for eight February nights, ideas moving from a laptop in 1019 to a live room in 995 to Alex Tumay’s desk in 9000 before sunrise. Most of what got tracked that week never made the album under the name it was cut under.
